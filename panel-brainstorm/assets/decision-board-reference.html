Top 5 — what to build

Ranked by panel convergence (how many personas independently proposed it), CPO verdict, and fit with what already exists in the code. Deciding a card decides every idea it covers; fine-tune individual ideas in the full list below.

#1 · Proactive

Sprout Briefing — Next Best Action M

Replace today’s greeting (“You have 9 pending items”) with a ranked plan: the top 3 things to do first, in order, each with a one-line why and an Open → button. Finish one, the next surfaces.

4 of 6 panelists proposed this independently — the strongest convergence in the whole panel. It reuses notificationsForView(), which already aggregates pending work per role.

Proposed by Marisol (Ops), Priya (Growth PM), Dex (Engineer), Jomar (new VA)
CPO check: notification items today carry only label + count — no age, deadline, or owner. The real work is enriching them with urgency metadata; reordering counts isn’t prioritization.
covers idea #1

1 Lock & close July payroll · due today Open →
2 Tier review — Maya · waiting 4 days Open →
3 Capacity flag — Theo underutilized Open →
…and 6 smaller things · show all
greeting → ranked plan
The chat’s first message becomes a prioritized briefing, not a badge count.
#2 · Navigation

Take Me There — wayfinding kit S

Every how-to answer ends in a button, not a paragraph. Sprout emits a typed route ID alongside the answer; the bubble renders Take me there →, navigates, and flashes the existing tour spotlight on the exact control when you arrive.

Same rails power “go to payroll archive” as a direct command, and a “show me” pulse on any button an answer names. This is the whole “help people who are lost” ask — and the spotlight machinery already exists.

Proposed by Wren (UX), Dex (Engineer), Jomar (new VA)
CPO check: the model must pick stable route IDs from one shared allow-list catalog (shared with tours + notifications) — never emit raw URLs.
covers idea #8

Take me there →
arrival spotlight on the nav item
Answers become one tap; the spotlight lands on the target when the page opens.
#3 · Context

Page-Aware Sprout S

Sprout knows what page you’re on before you ask. Opening the panel shows a contextual chip — “What can I do on this page?” — and answers say “you’re already here, click Approve on the row” instead of directions from the lobby.

Add Summarize this page (“who’s overloaded on Workload right now”) and short-window chat memory so “what about the second one?” just works. Today the model gets neither the page nor any history.

Proposed by Dex, Wren, Marisol, Kade (AI Strategist)
CPO check: pathname is cheap and safe; record-level context needs a server-authorized provider. Start memory as a bounded ephemeral window (last 4–6 turns), not new tables.
covers ideas #14 · #15 · #16
✦ What can I do on this page?
You’re on Workload — Theo has 9 open tasks, Maya has 1. Want me to suggest a rebalance?
knows the route + live data
One contextual chip per page replaces the same four static suggestions everywhere.
#4 · Action

Sprout Actions — draft, don’t just tell L

Graduate the hidden Bypass machinery into a first-class, role-scoped feature. Say “get someone to follow up with the Meridian client” — Sprout drafts the task (title, least-busy assignee, priority, due date) and opens the real form pre-filled. You adjust and send.

Safe everyday actions (update my task status, claim a task) get the propose → confirm → audit flow that already exists; after any confirmed action Sprout shows the result with an Open result link.

Proposed by Marisol, Dex, Kade
CPO check: the execute endpoint currently trusts client-sent tool + args. Opening this beyond admins requires actor-bound, expiring proposal tokens and a server-side per-role tool allow-list — that’s the real L in the estimate.

#5 · Coaching

Get Set Up — Week-1 companion M

Retire the 18-stop lecture tour. New users get a per-role checklist that checks itself off from real signals — first task opened, first check-in submitted, first claim — with a progress ring on the mascot until it’s done. Resumable, never lost on reload.

Pair it with tappable reassurance chips (“Is it normal I haven’t gotten a task yet?”) and a tier encourager that frames the hours bar as progress toward eligibility, warm and concrete.

Proposed by Jomar, Wren, Priya
CPO check: derive completion from authoritative events, not stored booleans; tie timing to onboarding state, not “Day 1/2/3”; never predict promotions — eligibility only.

Cheapest first move (the CPO’s pick): ship the 👍/👎 feedback loop (#30 below) before any of these — it’s an S, and it’s the measurement spine that tells you whether everything above is actually working.
